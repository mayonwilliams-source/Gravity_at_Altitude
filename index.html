What is k? k is the distance decay exponent. It controls how strongly distance suppresses passenger demand between two airports.

At k = 0, distance has no effect — centrality is determined almost entirely by airport size. Global mega-hubs dominate.

As k increases, nearby airports are increasingly favoured over distant ones. The ranking shifts away from globally large hubs toward airports that are geographically central within H3's own network.

At k = 1.0, the centrality ranking is essentially independent of raw airport size — it reflects pure network geometry.

Real-world aviation gravity models typically sit around k = 1.0–1.5.
